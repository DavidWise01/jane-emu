The frozen files are embedded byte for byte and never edited. The step rule is the frozen one: no release, no reset, no re-entry, so a finished instance stays finished and "New instance" makes a separate one. The inference wave. Each frame one forward pass travels inward: sparks leave the six RAM inputs at the edges, land on the ring, and fall into the singleton at the centre. The ring is the 16 hidden units bent into a circle, four arcs for the four carrier quads (WEAK/spatial, MEDIUM/temporal, STRONG/timing, FUSION/coincidence); it bulges where a unit fires. FUSION neurons gate on WEAK×MEDIUM: they fire only when spatial and temporal signals coincide. Its colour turns with the output, its spin follows the gap, and a ripple leaves the centre when the score byte really rises. Below, a spectrum analyzer shows the 16 units with falling peak caps and a scope traces the singleton over time. The faint glow behind is the field the net has learned. Around the ring sits the reservoir: six spokes of four dots, a fixed, untrained echo of the console's rare events, each held at four time-scales (inner dot fades in half a second, outer in ten). It is the net's only memory. Ping strikes it and times the echo; Drain empties it every frame, the known-bad for memory. Scramble labels is the known-bad for learning: the ring goes red and the scope turns to noise. Plain version: the little brain's thought, drawn as a ripple and a heartbeat line. Ornament: feedback trails, colour cycling, spark paths. Live values: ring shape, bar heights, scope line. Numbers are in Notes.

Three tracks run in parallel. Tell chains learn which attack follows which — if tell A always precedes tell B by 40 frames, Jane can prepare for B the moment A fires, before B's own tell shows up. Counter discovery tracks which punch type does damage after each dodge — replacing blind face jabs with targeted strikes. Phase detection watches the opponent's attack rate over 10-second windows — when the opponent speeds up or slows down, Jane knows something changed. Rules are forward-chained: chains feed predictions, counters feed the act() method, phases feed strategy. All state survives save/load.

Every memory the agent forms is a typed leaf in a domain-separated SHA-256 Merkle tree (0x00 leaf / 0x01 node). An OBS leaf stores the four wheel readings; a CAL leaf binds a belief revision to the observation it refined and never rewrites the raw sample. Each append re-pins the head; verify rehashes the whole prefix to it. Chrome is chroma, not chromodynamics — no quantum state here; the singlet is the fixed origin, not a QCD color-singlet. The wavelength wheel is an illustrative nm→sRGB swatch, approximate because monochromatic light lies outside the sRGB gamut (the spectral locus sits outside the RGB triangle); it is not spectroscopy. Phase is a true fraction of one plan cycle. Each OBS leaf also records why the agent acted — the strategy it chose and the scores it imagined — so the ledger proves the reason, not just the event, and tampering the reason breaks the hash. Beyond the full prefix rehash, an RFC 6962 consistency proof (compact: ~log₂N hashes, not the whole tree) proves the ledger only ever grew from a pinned checkpoint — change any earlier memory and that proof fails too. Honest limit that remains: this is one in-page tree, not an independent witness quorum.

  

  

    
LIT i13 BRAIN: Jane's neural net runs through the i13 interpreter. CUBIT MLP (30→16→8, Q8 fixed-point) + 10×10 MESH NET + PRIMER, all pure i13 — no host functions, no mutable globals, recursion only. 36 functions, 307 lines. Verified against independent Python reference: 450/450 crosscheck (MLP forward 200/200, Learning 100/100, Mesh 100/100, Primer 50/50).

    
Plain version. A pretend Nintendo runs inside this page. A little robot plays it. It never looks at the picture — only at the console's box of 2048 numbers. It finds which number is "me", which only-goes-up number is the score, and which number sits next to it right before something good or bad. Then, to choose, it runs a whole copy of the console forward in its head under a few different plans and keeps the one the numbers liked best.

    
The cycle. SEE reads the numbers. REMEMBER names them. IMAGINE rolls a copy of the console forward under each strategy. DO plays the winner. Then the real game moves and it SEEs again — the loop closes through the game. Each hop is one-way (feed-forward); the whole thing is a cycle. "Push backwards" tries to shove data back up a hop and is refused.

    
Two games, one agent. CATCH the ball or DODGE it — the switch tells the agent nothing. It reads the same bytes and its imagination picks: chase for catch, avoid for dodge. It is never told which game it is in or which direction to move.

    LIT Reservoir: fixed and untrained, 6 channels at 4 time-scales, driven only by RAM bytes that rarely change. Trained on one half, frozen, scored on the other: "was there a cue in the last 1 s / 3 s" 0.999 / 0.999 with it, 0.50 without it and 0.50 drained. Fed two clocks like the ones it fell for on a real cartridge, it keeps neither (1 dissents logged). The real score byte is still found on 5 of 5 seeds on CATCH and 5 of 5 on DODGE.

    LIT Rehearsal, on the mock ring: on a busier ring over 72,000 frames, plain learning takes 75 hits, floors the rival 18 times and throws 3,007 punches; with rehearsal it takes 9 hits, floors the rival 25 times and throws 1,610 punches, 1,568 of them openings the copy said would land. Known-bad: a copy whose opponent punches at the wrong time is caught by the audit after 3 wrong calls and switched off, and play falls back to plain learning.

    LIT New-opponent detection: when the rival's health byte refills from below 15% to above 80% of its observed maximum, tells are decayed (×0.5) rather than cleared — if it's the same opponent, the remnant rebuilds in 1–2 encounters; if it's a genuinely new opponent, the old tells fade since they stop getting confirmed. Tells with n<0.5 are pruned as noise. The 300-frame guard stops it from firing on normal health fluctuations.

    LIT Dynamic cycling: rolling 10-second checkpoint ring — one snapshot per second, 10 deep. The instant Jane gets knocked down (wasDown edge, not a timer), she reviews the ring and picks the checkpoint with the highest HP (the moment before things went wrong). Restores from there with a shifted random seed so she tries different dodge timings. Fight-start auto-save kept as hard fallback. Ring clears on new fight and on reload to prevent stale re-picks.

Primed cartridge (mapper 9 only). On this one cartridge the agent is no longer told nothing. From public documentation it is given three things: the byte holding its own health ($0391), the byte holding the opponent's ($0398), and the rule that a boxer on the canvas gets up by tapping A and B fast. Everything else it must learn from RAM: which rare event is a tell, how long after it the hit lands, and which answer avoids it (dodge left at once, dodge left late, dodge right late, or block). After a clean avoid it throws jabs. Here the 1UP number is its health and a streak is punches landed without being hit. It also rehearses: a second copy of the console is replayed against the real one, and only if every byte matches is it trusted. Then, when an event foresees a hit at the same delay twice, the boxer plays each answer forward in the copy and takes the one that loses least, before the real punch lands. It keeps punching until 24 frames before the answer is due. Between punches coming at it, it looks for openings the same way and throws only what the copy says will land. If answers the copy called clean keep getting hit for real, rehearsal switches itself off. When the rival's health refills from low (a new opponent), tells are decayed (×0.5) rather than cleared — if it's the same opponent, the remnant rebuilds fast; if it's a genuinely new opponent, the old tells fade since they stop getting confirmed. After a dodge, a chain prediction says what comes next; the boxer holds block during that predicted window (a guard). If the predicted tell actually fires while guarding, the guard is promoted to a full dodge — exploration is skipped, and the dodge fires immediately. Plain version: it learns "when that number flips, a punch is coming in half a second, so move now", and it can practise the move in its head first. After dodging, if it expects the next punch and that punch arrives while it's already braced, it skips the flinch and ducks straight away. Its body remembers which dodge direction worked for each attack pattern, so when a new round starts it doesn't have to re-learn from scratch — it tries what worked last time first. If it gets hit three times in a row at roughly the same fast rhythm, it recognises a stagger combo and holds block for 30 frames to break the chain. The three core subsystems are bound together: the reservoir's energy level (how busy rare RAM events are) feeds into tell confidence — when the reservoir is active, exploration is skipped because the tell is almost certainly real. The CUBIT's pain prediction gates JIT combos — a dubious combo is vetoed when the CUBIT sees a calm state. And successful counter punches after dodges are fed back into the CUBIT's training as positive examples, teaching it that post-dodge counter states are scoring opportunities. Plain version: if the game's memory is buzzing with rare changes, a tell is real, so she acts instead of exploring. If her gut (the CUBIT) says it's calm and a combo has been unreliable, she drops the combo and tries something simpler. And every time a counter punch after a dodge actually scores, her gut learns that "just-dodged" states are good ones to attack in. After a dodge, she escalates her counter punches: she starts with the type that worked best against this tell, and if no damage lands within 9 frames she switches to the second-best, then the third-best. If one scores, she locks onto that type for the rest of the counter window. Plain version: instead of throwing the same punch over and over after dodging, she tries her best punch first, and if it doesn't work she quickly switches to plan B, then plan C — and once something lands, she sticks with it. She also tracks the actual damage delta — how many health points each counter punch type takes off the opponent, not just whether it connected. This means she learns to prefer face punches (which do more damage) over body punches (which do less) for the same tell. Plain version: she now knows the difference between a jab that nicks and a hook that hurts, and picks the one that hurts. When a counter punch lands during the window, the window extends by 8 frames (capped at 40 total) so she can press the advantage while the opponent is still reeling. Plain version: if her punch connects, she gets a few bonus frames to keep swinging instead of the clock running out mid-combo.
